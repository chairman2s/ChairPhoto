The reason: headroom

A bright sky, three ways. The camera's JPEG has already decided the sky is white; the RAW still knows better.

As shot camera preview

The whites zone is full: the sky is one flat value.

Exposure −1 on the camera preview (today)

Everything gets darker, the sky becomes grey — the detail was gone before the file reached ChairPhoto.

Exposure −1 on the RAW (this feature)

The clouds come back and the whites mass spreads into lights and highlights — the tone strip finally shows the photo, not the preview.
Sensor clipping overlay — an optional overlay marking where the RAW itself is clipped. That is the only white that is gone for good; everything else is headroom. It answers a different question from the output-clipping warning and stays separate from it.
